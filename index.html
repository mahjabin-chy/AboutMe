Purpose and Long-Term Goals

My long-term research goal is to empower learners and promote learning through constructing and making in makerspaces, shifting pedagogical practices from rote memorization to authentic learning environments. These environments foster autonomy, agency, generativity, and collaborative learning opportunities.

Theoretical Framework

My research is grounded in collaborative learning, generativity, and learner agency. I design learning experiences where learners collaborate to generate artifacts, as exemplified in my dissertation study where elementary English language learners used Scratch programming to create digital games based on non-fiction texts. Drawing from constructionist theory (Papert, 1993), I advocate for learning environments that intrinsically motivate learners to create artifacts, fostering autonomy and authority over goals, processes, roles, and outcomes.

Domains & Methodological Expertise

My research spans curriculum and instruction, literacy, learning experience design, faculty development, teacher education, and learning sciences. I utilize quantitative, qualitative, and mixed-methods research, including semantic network analysis and design-based research (DBR) through the IDEALS Lab and IDEATE community.

Core Teaching Pillars

1. Collaboration & Generativity: Utilizing reflective practice (Schön, 1983) and formative feedback loops to drive student agency.
2. Critical Evaluation: Applying Bloom's Taxonomy and De Bono's Six Thinking Hats to move beyond rote learning to real-world application.
3. Embracing Diverse Perspectives: Fostering safe, inclusive environments through experiential cultural icebreakers and active listening.

I foster an environment in which students share intellectual excitement and find ways to think critically about topics and apply their findings to their lives. As a non-native English speaker, I approach teaching as an exciting dialogue rather than a one-way lecture, cultivating an approachable and friendly classroom.

In my ESL Methods courses, I integrate real-world case studies, field experiences, and active reflection. For example, to cultivate empathy for English language learners, I teach the first 10 minutes of a second-class session in Bengali. This immersive experience allows pre-service teachers to directly experience the cognitive load and psychological state of low-proficiency language learners.
